👥 Math Crew — What Do They Think?

🧭 Atlas

I noticed every conversion uses the same move: multiply the fraction by the conversion number. It didn't matter if I was converting feet, pounds, or yards — the multiplication step never changed, only the conversion number did.

🔧 Felix

I checked my answer by working backwards. Two thirds of a foot is 8 inches, so 8 inches out of 12 should give back two thirds of a foot, and it does.

🎨 Hazel

I picture a foot-long strip cut into equal pieces, laid right on top of an inch ruler. Whatever fraction is shaded, I can just read off how many inches that shaded part covers.

📝 Cora

The precise term is conversion number — it's how many of the smaller unit fit inside one of the bigger unit. Multiplying a fraction by the conversion number gives an equivalent measurement in the smaller unit.

Worked Example
Return to the warm-up situation.

Back to the ribbon: 23 of a foot. How many inches is that?

  1. 1 foot = 12 inches. Draw a bar for 1 foot with a 12-inch strip under it.
  2. Cut the foot into 3 equal parts. Each third lines up with 12 ÷ 3 = 4 inches.
  3. Shade 2 thirds. They line up with 4 + 4 = 8 inches.
  4. 23 ft = 8 in.

Check: 23 × 12 = 243 = 8. Now build it in the tool and write the answer in a sentence.

Socratic question: What part of the model proves the answer, not just shows it?
