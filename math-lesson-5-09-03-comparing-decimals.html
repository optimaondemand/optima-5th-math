Essential Question

How can we tell which decimal is greater?

More digits does not mean a greater number. The places decide.

Today's Objectives

You will…

  • Compare decimals to thousandths using place value and <, > or =.
  • Order decimals from least to greatest and from greatest to least.
  • Explain why more digits does not always mean a greater number.

LEARN IT Compare place by place

👥 Math Crew: four ways to compare 3.528 and 3.582

Ones and tenths are the same. Hundredths: 2 < 8. So 3.528 < 3.582.
528 thousandths < 582 thousandths.
On the number line, 3.582 is farther right.
3.582 > 3.528. Same numbers, read the other way.

📐 Hemiunu’s Comparing Rules

Ones2.Tenths4Hundredths1Thousandths6

Ones2.Tenths4Hundredths6Thousandths1
  • Line up the decimal points, so each place sits under the same place.
  • Compare from the left, the greatest place first.
  • The first place where the digits are different decides which number is greater.
  • Write a 0 in an empty place. It does not change the value: 1.5 = 1.500.
  • On a number line, the greater number is farther right.

My scribes made three mix-ups that kids often make too. Study the solved example first, then fix each one.

🔍 Study It: 5.07 or 5.069?

Already solved. Read both ways, then fix the mix-ups.

Way 1: line up the places
Ones5.Tenths0Hundredths7Thousandths0

Ones5.Tenths0Hundredths6Thousandths9

Write 5.07 as 5.070. Ones and tenths are the same. Hundredths: 7 > 6. So 5.07 > 5.069.

Way 2: count thousandths

5.070 = 5 and 70 thousandths.

5.069 = 5 and 69 thousandths.

70 > 69, so 5.07 > 5.069, even though 5.069 has more digits.

🔍 Study It: order 2.15, 2.105 and 2.5

Write them with three places: 2.150, 2.105, 2.500. The ones are the same. Tenths: 1, 1, 5. So 2.5 is the greatest. Compare the other two at the hundredths: 5 > 0, so 2.15 > 2.105.
