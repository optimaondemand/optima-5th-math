Readiness Check: Equivalent decimals
A zero at the end can show the same amount in smaller pieces.

0.6 and 0.60 name the same amount. The 0 at the end does not add more shaded space. It shows 6 tenths as 60 hundredths.

Centerpiece: Decimal Strip Aligner
Watch 0.6 become 0.60 before you compare it to 0.57.
👀

Goal: Do not compare 6 and 57 like whole numbers. First, make the pieces the same size. Then compare the shaded amount.

1. Look at the original strips
2. Predict first
3. Split tenths into hundredths
4. Choose and check a symbol
Step 1 — See the decimals as they are

0.6 means 6 tenths. 0.57 means 5 tenths and 7 hundredths. The bars are the same whole size, but the pieces shown are not matched yet.

0.66 tenths shaded
This strip is split into 10 tenths. Six tenths are shaded.
?
0.575 tenths + 7 hundredths
This strip shows 5 full tenths and 7 hundredths inside the next tenth.
Step 2 — Prediction: Which decimal do you think is greater before subdividing? Why?
🧠

What to watch: When you split 0.6 into hundredths, the bar does not get longer. Each shaded tenth breaks into 10 smaller hundredths. That is why 0.6 can also be written as 0.60.

Why does adding a zero to 0.6 not change its value?
What You Just Saw

Decimals are easiest to compare when the pieces are the same size. You can write 0.6 as 0.60 without changing its value. Then you compare 60 hundredths and 57 hundredths.

Worked Example
Compare 2.305 and 2.35.

Both numbers have 2 ones. Compare the decimal part by matching thousandths: 2.35 is the same as 2.350. Now compare 305 thousandths with 350 thousandths. Since 350 thousandths is greater, 2.35 > 2.305.

Practice
