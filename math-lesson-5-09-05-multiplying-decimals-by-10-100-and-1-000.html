Multiplying Decimals by 10, 100, and 1,000

Big Question: How can a decimal become 10, 100, or 1,000 times larger without “moving” the decimal point?
Today’s Mission
Use the Decimal Slide Mat to show why digits slide left when a decimal is multiplied by powers of 10.

Key Concept

Multiplying by 10, 100, or 1,000 makes a decimal value larger. Each digit moves to a larger place. The decimal point does not move; the digits move around it.

Worked Example
0.248 × 100

Multiplying by 100 means two left slides. The 2 tenths become 2 tens? Pause: no. One slide makes 2 tenths into 2 ones. A second slide makes them 2 tens. The full number becomes 24.8.

So 0.248 × 100 = 24.8.
