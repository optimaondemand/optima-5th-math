Today's Mission
Build a three-row ratio bar model, compare two rows at a time, and explain how all three rows stay connected.
❓
Essential Question
How can one ratio compare three quantities at once?

A two-part ratio compares two things, like green to gold. A three-part ratio compares three things in one linked model.

Today you will see that 2:3:5 means three rows are connected: 2 parts, 3 parts, and 5 parts.

🎯
Today's Objectives
You will…
📊Build a three-row ratio bar model with equal-sized parts.
🔍Compare any two quantities from a three-part ratio.
💬Explain how scaling all three rows keeps the same comparison.

Interactive Model: Triple Ratio Builder
Build three linked rows, then choose which two rows to compare.
What this model is trying to do: It keeps three quantities connected in one picture.Step 1: Build the base ratio 2:3:5. Step 2: Scale all rows by the same factor. Step 3: Click two rows to compare only those two, without forgetting the whole three-part ratio.
Trail Team Ratio: Hikers : Cyclists : Paddlers

2

Hikers : Cyclists : Paddlers = 2 : 3 : 5All three rows are part of the same ratio.
1. OrderHikers, cyclists, paddlers.
2. Parts2 parts, 3 parts, 5 parts.
3. ScaleMultiply every row by the same number.
4. PairChoose any two rows to compare.
📐 What the model showed

2:3:5 is a three-row relationship. The first number belongs to the first row, the second number belongs to the second row, and the third number belongs to the third row.

When the group grows, every row is multiplied by the same number. If the scale is ×2, the rows become 4, 6, and 10. The counts changed, but the ratio pattern stayed the same.
